## Missing information and decisions

- Insert the Company's final registered name, company type, ORC number, TIN, incorporation date, and registered office. The current draft cannot be signed for a non-existent company.
- Insert the Founder and Investor legal identities, addresses, identification/registration numbers, nationalities, beneficial owners, and source-of-funds information.
- Agree the investment amount and currency, pre-money and post-money valuation, share class, number of new shares, subscription price per share, individual percentage, and whether percentages use issued or fully diluted capitalization. Confirm mathematically that post-money equals pre-money plus the investment, price multiplied by new shares equals the investment, and all investors together receive no more than 10.00% post-round. Ghanaian company shares have no par value under Act 992, so the stated price must be the subscription price, not a purported par value.
- Obtain and reconcile the current capitalization table, constitution, issued shares, any reserved shares/options, existing shareholder rights, and the Company's authority to issue the proposed shares. The 10% round allocation is a fundraising intention, not proof that shares are presently authorized or available.
- Define the class rights and any separate investor protections in definitive subscription and shareholders' agreements. Consider information rights, pro-rata participation, transfer restrictions, reserved matters, founder/IP arrangements, tag/drag rights, confidentiality, dispute resolution, and closing/termination mechanics without creating a Founder personal guarantee.
- Finalize the use-of-funds percentages, payment account, closing date, conditions precedent, KYC/AML process, and treatment of transaction expenses, taxes, and stamp duty.

## Required Ghana closing actions

1. Incorporate the Company in Ghana as the intended private company limited by shares, file beneficial-ownership information, adopt an appropriate constitution, establish the registered office and statutory registers, and ensure that all platform intellectual property and contracts are properly owned or licensed by the Company.
2. Have Ghanaian counsel verify the cap table and constitution. Under Act 992, a private company restricts share transfers, limits members, and may not invite the public to acquire its shares. New shares require valuable consideration, authorization consistent with the constitution, a specific ordinary resolution, and observance or lawful waiver/decline of existing shareholders' pre-emption rights. If the approved share structure, constitution, or class rights do not accommodate the round, complete the necessary shareholder/class approvals and ORC filings first.
3. Restrict discussions and documents to individually identified prospective investors until counsel confirms the securities position. An electronic advertisement or broad circulation can be an invitation to the public. A public offer would engage the Act 992 prospectus/public-company regime and section 145 of Act 929/SEC registration requirements. Do not market the 10% allocation publicly on the assumption that it is an exempt private placement; counsel should determine the transaction's classification and whether Act 929 as amended by Act 1062, the SEC Regulations, 2003 (L.I. 1728), or any current SEC filing, approval, licensed-intermediary, disclosure, or other requirement applies.
4. At Closing, approve the allotment through the board and members as required, sign definitive subscription/shareholders' documents, receive cleared funds into the Company's account, and record the allotment. Membership arises upon entry in the register of members, not merely on signing this draft.
5. After allotment, submit the prescribed return of issue (Form 7) to the Office of the Registrar of Companies within 28 days, update the register of members and beneficial-ownership records and make related notifications within the applicable periods, and issue the share certificate within two months. Confirm current forms and filing practice with the ORC and counsel.
6. Ask a Ghanaian tax adviser whether the definitive instruments must be stamped and how the capital contribution and share issue should be treated. If any Investor is non-Ghanaian, obtain advice before Closing on the Ghana Investment Promotion Centre Act, applicable minimum foreign-capital rules or exclusions, banking/Bank of Ghana evidence, registration, and repatriation requirements.
